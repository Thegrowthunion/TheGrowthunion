How Much Does a Website Really Cost in Delhi NCR in 2026?

Quotes range from ₹5,000 to ₹5 lakh for what sounds like the same thing. Here's what actually drives that gap — and what you should realistically expect to pay.

Ask five people in Delhi NCR "how much should a website cost" and you'll get five wildly different answers — ₹5,000 from a freelancer on Fiverr, ₹50,000 from a local agency, ₹5 lakh from an enterprise firm. All of them are technically telling the truth. None of them are telling you the whole story.

The honest answer is that website pricing isn't really about the number of pages or how it looks. It's about what's actually built underneath — and that's exactly what most quotes never explain clearly.

What You're Actually Paying For

Price RangeWhat You Typically GetWho It's Right For
₹5,000 – ₹15,000Template-based site, basic pages, limited customisation, often no SEO foundationA very early-stage business that just needs an online presence to exist
₹15,000 – ₹50,000Custom-built pages, mobile-optimised, SEO-ready structure, lead capture formsMost small and mid-sized Delhi NCR businesses ready to generate real enquiries
₹50,000 – ₹2 lakhFully custom design, advanced functionality, integrations (CRM, payment, booking)Growing businesses with specific operational needs beyond a brochure site
₹2 lakh+Enterprise-grade builds, custom applications, high-traffic infrastructureLarger companies with complex, high-stakes digital operations
The real question isn't "how much does a website cost." It's "what does my business actually need it to do."

The Hidden Cost Most Quotes Don't Mention

A shockingly common pattern in Delhi NCR: a business pays ₹8,000–₹15,000 for a website, only to discover a year later that it was never built to be found on Google in the first place — no proper meta tags, no schema markup, no mobile optimisation done correctly, sometimes not even a sitemap. The site exists, technically. It just doesn't do the one job a business website actually needs to do: bring in enquiries.

This is the gap between a website that "looks fine" and a website that's genuinely SEO-ready from day one — proper title tags and meta descriptions on every page, schema markup so Google understands what the business does, fast load times, and a clear path for a visitor to actually make contact.

What a Fair Price Actually Looks Like

For most small and mid-sized businesses across Gurgaon, Delhi, Noida, and Panipat, a genuinely well-built 5-page business website — Homepage, Products/Services, About Us, Contact, and a Gallery or Portfolio page — with WhatsApp integration, a working enquiry form, mobile-first design, and SEO fundamentals built in from the start, typically falls in the ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 range as a one-time cost.

Below that range, be specifically cautious about what's excluded — SEO readiness, mobile optimisation, and ongoing support are the three things most commonly stripped out of ultra-cheap quotes to hit a lower headline price.

Questions Worth Asking Before You Pay Anyone

Does the price include mobile optimisation, or is that a separate add-on? Is basic SEO (meta tags, sitemap, schema) included, or extra? How many rounds of revisions are included before additional charges apply? Who owns the domain and hosting account once the project is done — you, or the developer?

Ongoing Costs Nobody Mentions Upfront

  • Domain renewal — typically ₹800–1,500/year, paid to the registrar, not the developer
  • Hosting — ₹3,000–10,000/year depending on traffic and platform
  • Maintenance — updates, security patches, and small content changes, often ₹2,000–5,000/month if not handled in-house

A website is not a one-time purchase the way a business card is. Budgeting only for the build cost and being surprised by renewal and maintenance costs a year later is one of the most common frustrations we hear from Delhi NCR business owners.

The Real Decision

The cheapest quote and the most expensive quote can both be the wrong choice for the same business. What actually matters is matching the build to what the business needs it to do — generate enquiries, rank on Google, or simply exist as a professional reference point. Getting honest about that upfront saves far more money than chasing the lowest number.

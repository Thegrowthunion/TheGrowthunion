Why Most GTM Strategies Fail Before They Even Launch

By Neeraj Grover · November 2026 · 4 min read

A launch plan is a calendar. A go-to-market strategy is a system. Most businesses confuse the two, and pay for it later.

What a launch plan actually is

A launch plan tells you what happens on which date: the post goes up on Monday, the ad goes live on Wednesday, the email goes out on Friday. It is useful. It is also not a strategy.

What a go-to-market strategy has to answer

A real strategy answers five questions before a single rupee is spent:

  • Who exactly are we selling to?
  • Why would they choose us over what they already use?
  • Where will they find us?
  • Who follows up with each enquiry, how fast, and with what?
  • How will we know in four weeks that it is working?

Where it usually breaks

Four failures show up again and again. There is no clear ideal customer, so the message tries to speak to everyone. Channels get picked before the message is tested. Nobody owns lead follow-up, so enquiries go cold. And there is no weekly review, so nothing improves.

What to do instead

Write your customer down in one paragraph. Test the message in real conversations before you put money behind it. Give every lead a named owner. Look at the numbers every week and change one thing at a time.

A strategy you can explain in five minutes is a strategy your team can actually run.
